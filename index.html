        How it works, data sources and assumptions

        Substitution rules. Capacitance and case size must match. Rated voltage can only go up. Dielectric can only go up (Y5V, X5R, X7R, C0G), and the operating temperature range can’t shrink. Tolerance can only get tighter. Grades aren’t a ladder: AEC-Q200 can replace commercial, but nothing replaces medical except a medical-grade series. End-of-life parts are ruled out. A changed or missing spec sends the part to engineering review.

        Engineering-review flags. Some risks aren’t a pass or fail on the datasheet, so each candidate lists them for the engineer, with no calculation: DC bias on X5R, X7R and Y5V parts that aren’t the same design as the original (class II ceramics lose capacitance under DC voltage, and the curve differs by manufacturer, rated voltage and height); height when the same case size comes in a different thickness, or the thickness isn’t known and the voltage rating changes; and termination on automotive boards, where flexible (soft) termination protects against board-flex cracking. Losing flexible termination on an automotive part fails the check outright. Flags never rule a part out; among otherwise equal parts, fewer flags rank first.

        Approval and supply. The approval path and production date are estimates from typical engineering review, sample validation, AML and PPAP durations; real requirements vary by company and customer. Supply is from stock when it covers the quantity, otherwise the factory lead time; you order while approval runs.

        Data. A part number gets live specs, stock and prices from Octopart (Nexar), with Mouser as fallback. When a listing leaves a spec out, it is read from the part number (Murata, Samsung, TDK, KEMET, Yageo and KYOCERA AVX formats); height and termination always come from the part number. If live data doesn’t answer within 5 seconds, the page falls back to a 31-part demo catalog with simulated stock, lead times and prices, and says so. Prices are in USD; MXN and EUR use the European Central Bank reference rate, for information only.

        The full rule set, with the file and function behind each rule, is in the methodology (opens in a new tab).

        Problem

        When a capacitor goes on allocation, finding an alternate means manual spec checks, supplier calls and an approval path nobody has sized, while the line waits.

        Approach

        Encode the substitution rules, flag what engineering must review, and combine live distributor data with an approval and supply plan for each candidate.

        Outcome

        A ranked shortlist with the reason each part passes, needs review or fails, when it can be in production, and what it costs against the original.
